# pages — Route-Level Page Components

## Purpose

Top-level page components — one per application route. Each page composes hooks and presentational components to deliver a full screen of the app. Pages are registered in `src/App.tsx`.

## Ownership

- `HomePage.tsx` / `.css` — Landing page. Minimal hero section with the Cadenza logo and a single CTA linking to `/upload`.
- `UploadPage.tsx` / `.css` — Score upload screen. Drag-and-drop or browse for a MusicXML file (`.xml`, `.musicxml`, `.mxl`). Hosts the `MidiPanel` so the user can connect a MIDI device before opening the score. Passes the selected `File`, `midiEnabled`, and `midiInputId` to `/score` via `react-router-dom` `location.state`.
- `ScorePage.tsx` / `.css` — The main score viewer, play-along surface, and step-by-step practice surface. Dynamically imports `@music-i18n/musicxml-player` (Player + OSMD renderer + Verovio converter). Owns the take lifecycle as an explicit state machine, `phase: 'idle' | 'countdown' | 'playing'`: a countdown holds the player while the musician gets to the keyboard, then the score plays and the keyboard is recorded, then the verdict posts itself. Composes `PlaybackBar`, `PracticeBar`, `PianoKeyboard`, `useMidi`, `useVerdict`, and `usePractice`. It also owns `keyboardOpen` (the drawn hint keyboard: opened when practice starts, closed on exit, toggled by the strip). It no longer computes any range for that keyboard: the whole instrument is drawn, always — see `components/AGENTS.md`. At the end of a take it does NOT list the seven rules any more: it paints the violated spots red on the score and names them on hover (see "At the end of a take the verdict is painted ON the score" below); only `ZeldaPage`, the demo, still prints the ✓/✗ list.
- `ZeldaPage.tsx` / `.css` — `/zelda`, a demonstration page written by hand: the Great Fairy Fountain score and a real recorded performance are embedded in `src/data/zelda-performance.ts`, no upload and no live MIDI. Its only control sends that performance to the RML service and shows the seven verdicts, which is why it imports `API_URL`/`VerdictMap` from `useVerdict` rather than the hook itself. It reuses `ScorePage.css` for the sheet container, and there is no MIDI input and no practice surface here, so nothing on this page may be read as the real flow
- `MetricsTestPage.tsx` / `.css` — REMOVED. It recomputed six metrics in TypeScript with thresholds hardcoded in the page (`LEGATO_GAP_MS = 30`, attack ≥ 80, chord 30 ms, pedal 200 ms), duplicating the ones inside the RML `.rml` specs. Two copies of the same number diverge as soon as one is edited. Verdicts come from the service via `useVerdict` and are rendered by `ScorePage`; a removed page must not be reintroduced as an in-browser reimplementation.

## Local Contracts

- Route → page mapping is defined in `src/App.tsx`: `/` → `HomePage`, `/upload` → `UploadPage`, `/score` → `ScorePage`, `/zelda` → `ZeldaPage` (lazy, like the other two heavy pages)
- Thresholds and verdicts live only in `RML_DOCS/RML_TESTS/metrics/*.rml`. No page may contain a numeric threshold that decides pass/fail
- `ScorePage` shows the verdict panel only after `verdict.status !== 'idle'`, and the analysis posts itself when the score ends: there is no "Analizza la prova" button, because waiting for it only added a way to forget it
- `ScorePage` starts a take by calling `midi.clearRecording()` then `player.rewind()` — without the reset, a second take concatenates onto the first and the aligner reads both as one performance
- The end of the score has no event: `PlayerState` is only `Stopped`/`Playing`/`Paused` and the player exposes no emitter, so the end is deduced in a `requestAnimationFrame` loop from `position >= duration - END_TOLERANCE_MS` (250 ms, without it the last millisecond is cut)
- `UploadPage` → `ScorePage` handoff contract via `location.state`:
  - `file: File` — the selected MusicXML file (required; `ScorePage` redirects to `/upload` if absent)
  - `midiEnabled: boolean` — whether MIDI was enabled on the upload page
  - `midiInputId: string | null` — the selected MIDI input device ID, if any
- `ScorePage` owns the `playerRef` and `osmdRef`; `osmdRef` is load-bearing, not a diagnostic leftover: the practice cursor walk and the end-of-take red marks both drive the OSMD cursors through it. Plain playback still needs no help from there — `followCursor: true` on the renderer and the player moves the cursor by itself
- Space bar toggles play/pause on `ScorePage` (suppressed while loading and during a take)
- A take and plain playback are mutually exclusive — pressing Play mid-take does nothing, Stop aborts the take
- The countdown overlay is `position: fixed` over the **viewport**, with a transparent background — an explicit correction (2026-11-06): it used to be `absolute; inset: 0` inside `.score-container`, which is as tall as the whole document, so the number sat at the middle of 250 m of score while the musician looks at whatever is on screen, and its frosted-glass veil covered everything. Now the digits float over exactly what is visible, readable via a white text-shadow halo because there is no backdrop any more
- The countdown counts **4, 3, 2, 1 — one second per number** (clarified by the user the same day: "intendevo un countdown da 4 a 1, ogni numero vale 1 secondo"), the way a conductor gives a full bar of cues. Each number gets a WebAudio tick (`playTick`, sine 1040 Hz); "Go" gets the 1560 Hz accent and playback starts on that same instant, so the piece's downbeat lands on the accented click. The earlier variants — 750 ms flat, then a step tied to the slider's tempo — were both too fast for the hand to reach the keyboard ("TROPPO veloce"), and a four-second-per-number reading was too slow; `COUNTDOWN_STEP_MS = 1000` / `COUNTDOWN_FROM = 4` carry the settled request. The first tick sounds from inside the click handler while the user gesture still authorises audio; the rest are scheduled in the timer callback, not in the effect body, where StrictMode would fire them twice (a double flam in dev). If WebAudio is unavailable the numbers still count — sound is a help, never a dependency
- The digit animation must occupy the WHOLE second of its number and never stop moving: its duration is passed inline from `COUNTDOWN_STEP_MS` (one source with the clicks, so they cannot diverge) and the keyframes keep opacity and scale drifting until the next digit takes over at ~0. The old 0.75 s pop ending at opacity 0.35 with `fill-mode: both` froze each half-transparent digit for the leftover quarter second — "appare, si freeza, poi arriva il successivo", an explicit complaint (2026-11-06). Do not re-tune the pop shorter than the step: that is exactly what made it freeze

### The two modes are separate on purpose

- `isPractice` switches between them. Practice NEVER touches the player: no `position`, no `requestAnimationFrame` loop, no time factor sent to the service. The score stands still and the cursor marks one step of the exercise (positioned by that step's `attack`, not by its index), which is why the temporal alignment of the normal mode is not needed here at all
- The two panels are mutually exclusive. The verdict panel is hidden during practice and the practice panel is hidden outside it: they judge the same performance in two different ways, and showing them together would suggest the end-of-piece verdict also applies to the step mode
- `stepStartRef` marks where the current step begins inside `midi.recording`. The recording is NOT cleared between steps, so without that boundary the twentieth step would carry the notes of the previous nineteen and `pitch-agreement` would judge them against the wrong step, producing invented violations
- The step boundary moves in an effect keyed on `practice.index`, not right after `submit`, because the hook decides the advance and reading its result immediately would give the stale value
- The submit effect fires when the set of pressed pitches CHANGES, not on every MIDI event: a note-off changes nothing a practice rule looks at, and requesting a verdict for each release would queue four requests for a chord instead of two

### The cursor in practice mode

- `moveCursorToStep` drives the OSMD cursor by hand. `followCursor` is on, but it follows the **player**, and in practice the player never starts — so nothing would ever move it
- It counts POSITIONS, and does not compare pitches. This is the second version of this function, and the change is forced by a measurement, not by taste.
- The first version searched for the voice whose notes matched the step's. In a real browser it NEVER matched. OSMD reports every note 36 semitones (three octaves) below the plan, uniformly across the piece, because the score carries an `<octave-shift type="down" size="8">` on the violin staff. Step 0 asks for `[46, 93]` and the matching voice says `[10, 57]`; step 1 asks for `[46, 86]` and the voice says `[10, 50]`
- Writing a `-36` in the code would be a bug dressed as a constant: it would change with the first score that lacks that sign. The height of a note is not a reliable link between the two sides
- What DOES hold is the POSITION, and it was measured on the whole piece: with no hand chosen, the iterator's stop `k` is the plan's step `k` — 128 of 128, with the measure matching 128 of 128. Both sides list the attacks in chronological order, so counting is enough and no pitch is read
- With a hand chosen, the position to reach is `steps[i].attack`, not `i`. The cursor walks **every** attack of the score; an exercise for one hand skips some. The service already sends `attack` inside each step, and passing `index` instead fails *silently*: the mark lands on a neighbouring note, and the student plays what they see
- The iterator makes 256 stops for 128 steps because the score has a repeat: the second pass is byte-for-byte the first. Practice stops at step 127, i.e. at the end of the first pass, and the count therefore stops at `stepIndex` instead of trying to reach the end of the score
- `cursorStepRef` holds where the cursor stopped, so each step walks forward instead of restarting. It resets only when moving backwards or when its position is unknown (`-1`)
- Bound: 6000 steps — not a musical limit, the point where giving up beats freezing the page
- Leaving practice hides the cursor; the normal mode's `followCursor` takes over again

### At the end of a take the verdict is painted ON the score

- An explicit request (2026-11-06): the verdict box that listed "what passed and what didn't" was useless feedback because it never said WHERE. Now the violated notes go **red on the score**, and hovering a red note shows which rules were not respected there — minimally, only their names
- The decisions (which points, which words) live in `hooks/scoreMarks.ts`; the page keeps only the geometry of reaching a point and painting it (`paintScoreMarks` / `paintNotehead` / `clearScoreMarks`, module-level in `ScorePage.tsx`)
- The walk counts: each location carries `attack` (the absolute attack index — see Service contract in `../../AGENTS.md`) and the page drives the OSMD cursor with `moveCursorToStep`, the same forward-only counting primitive practice mode uses and that was measured 128/128 there. Pitch is never read for painting either: octave-shift makes height meaningless across the two sides, exactly as in practice mode
- Once at a stop, the cursor's own image gives the x and the system-band; the painted targets are the `g.vf-notehead` groups whose centre falls on that perpendicular — the whole chord at that instant, on either staff. The acceptance window is NOT an absolute pixel number: it is measured from the spacing between neighbouring notehead columns in the view (zoom-proof), and if nothing falls inside, the point is declared "missed" and named in the summary line instead of accusing a neighbour note
- The tooltip is a `<title>` SVG child: Chromium renders it as the native hover tooltip, no positioning code, and `g.vf-notehead` carries `pointer-events="bounding-box"` so the whole head area reacts (both measured on the real DOM, not assumed)
- Painting saves every path's original `fill`/`stroke` in a `MarkState`; closing the verdict or starting a new take restores them (the marking effect clears before it repaints), so red from take one never accuses take two. VexFlow groups are re-created on every re-render — after `zoom` changes the marks are simply repainted by the next take, and forced-colors/HC modes lean on the tooltip text, not the hue
- The panel under the score keeps exactly the lines `summaryLines` produces: where the red spots are ("N spots marked in the score — hover a red note…"), and only what no red can say: violations without a point, monitors that never started. The ✓/✗ list is gone here and lives only on `/zelda` (demo)
- The page scrolls to the FIRST painted spot when marks exist (the verdict that says where it happened should not make you hunt), and back to the panel otherwise

### The practice strip sits above the playback pill

- `PracticeBar` is one fixed row, 2.4 rem tall, sitting directly above the pill (`bottom: calc(1.5rem + 5rem + 0.6rem)`) and sharing its width and radius. Its whole content: the verdict dot + word, the keyboard toggle (which carries in its tooltip everything the counters used to say — step number, bar, key names, excluded presses, suspended rules), the two hand switches **L** / **R** and the **D** chip
- `ScorePage` owns the two L/R switches as state (`handsOn`) and maps them through `handSelection`: a call to `practice.setHand` happens only when the mapping differs from the hand the plan was built with (every `setHand` re-requests the plan, so an equal-value call would rebuild the exercise for nothing). With every drawn letter off the session is **held, not re-planned**: no network call goes out, step submissions are skipped (presses stay in the recording and are judged at resume as extras of the current step), and `PianoKeyboard` hides — lit keys asking for notes while both hands are off would be a picture that lies. Resuming onto the same hand costs zero requests because the kept plan is still the right one. Starting or leaving practice resets both switches to lit
- A switched-off hand also greys **its staff on the score** (the second half of the same 2026-11-07 request: "when L or R are off the respective staff becomes grey like the button; if not the whole staff at least its notes"). The whole staff is possible and that is what happens: OSMD wraps each hand's rendered measures in one `g.staffline` group whose id suffix names the side — `-1` upper/right, `-2` lower/left, measured on the real DOM 2026-11-07, the same kind of DOM-side fact the cursor walk leans on and never a note height. WHO dims is `staffGray`'s decision in `practiceSteps.ts` (right switch off ⇒ upper staff, left off ⇒ lower; one-staff file ⇒ its only staff exactly when paused; a file with no staff numbers ⇒ never, because there the switches change no exercise and a grey staff would lie); the page only toggles an `osmd-staff-off` class on those groups. The style is a group `filter: grayscale + fade`, not per-path colors: restoring is removing one class, so this paint never collides with the end-of-take red marks' save/restore of `fill`/`stroke`, and a window resize that re-renders simply reapplies it. Outside practice no staff is ever dimmed, and leaving practice clears every group — normal mode must not show a hand nobody switched off
- Both this row and the pill have a **solid** `--bg-secondary` background and no `backdrop-filter`. They float over the score, and while they were 0.85-alpha glass the notes showed through the buttons and the controls were hard to read; that was an explicit complaint, and an opacity check is part of the browser probe now. Since both bars are the same colour, the border between them is what keeps them two objects — do not raise one bar's alpha and leave the other transparent
- The keyboard hint `PianoKeyboard` is fixed directly above the strip (its own `bottom` adds the strip's height), never inline in the flow: it must not push the score or resize with scroll, and it stays out of the strip's row so the strip stays one line. It is display-only (`pointer-events: none`) — the keyboard that matters is under the student's hands
- The box the strip replaced was `position: sticky; top: 0` over the score, 207 px closed and 471 px open, two columns of keys and verdicts. It hid the first systems — which is where the cursor is — and an explicit request asked for the minimum instead
- It still renders *before* `.score-container` in the DOM. As a trailing element it used to land 2163 px down the document, two and a half screens below the fold
- The waiting and error states reuse the same row (`.practice-bar.practice-notice`, styles here in `ScorePage.css`) so nothing jumps when the plan turns out to be ready or a hand change is being re-planned. During those waits no keyboard is drawn: with no step loaded there is nothing to light up, and an all-dark keyboard would be a picture that lies
- The strip must never grow again: the cursor and the lit keys are the two things looked at together, and the whole exercise is the relation between them. A number was removed from here because it never said *which* key; the drawn keyboard says it without taking a second line
- The keyboard is drawn **whole, all 88 keys (A0–C8)** — the user asked for every key on 2026-11-05, and it is the component's own default, not something `ScorePage` feeds it any more. A plan-wide window was tried first and failed twice over: its `useMemo` had swapped extremes (`min` held `Math.max`, invisible to `tsc`) so it drew one octave where the piece spans four, dropping the step's own second key off the drawing; and even fixed correctly it answers the wrong question — a cropped keyboard never says where the piece sits on the instrument, which is how a pianist orients. The width (76 rem, ~23 px per white key) is the component's answer to the "six pixels per key" worry that motivated cropping

## Work Guidance

- The `@music-i18n/musicxml-player`, `opensheetmusicdisplay`, and `webmidi` libraries are dynamically or lazily loaded where possible to keep the initial bundle small
- `ScorePage` uses module-level `let` refs for dynamically-imported library classes (`Player`, `OpenSheetMusicDisplayRenderer`, `VerovioConverter`) — these are populated on first load
- Verovio converter uses the `Bravura` font to avoid Leipzig font-loading errors
- Loading states must show a spinner and message; errors must offer a "Try Another File" path back to `/upload`
- Keep the practice surface in `ScorePage` rather than a new route: it needs the same player, the same MIDI hook instance, and the same rendered score, and a second route would duplicate the 11 MB player chunk

## Verification

- `npm run lint` — ESLint
- `npm run build` — TypeScript type-check + Vite build
- Manual: upload a `.musicxml` file, verify rendering, then press "Play along" and check that the countdown runs **over the part of the page you are looking at** (scroll down first — the number must float there, transparent, notes readable underneath, counting 4-3-2-1 one second per number with a tick on each and the accented tick on "Go" doubling as the piece's downbeat), the score plays, and the verdict arrives without pressing anything — at the end the notes where the take fell short are red on the score, hovering one shows only the rule names, and the strip under the score states how many spots were marked (or that every checked rule was respected); "Close" and "Play again" remove the reds cleanly
- Manual (practice): press "Practice", check the plan loads, then play a step. The drawn keyboard must appear by itself, lit on exactly the keys the step asks for; it must turn green under the fingers before the verdict word arrives (that is the local status, not the service); pressing an extra key must NOT block the exercise; the next step's keys must light up as soon as the step completes; and the title of the keyboard toggle must carry the step number, the bar, the key names and the three suspended rules with their reason
- Manual (hands): L and R are **two independent switches** (explicit request 2026-11-07). From both lit, press **L** — it alone goes grey, its staff on the score dims into the same dead grey with it, and the exercise becomes the right hand's; press **L** again — both lit, whole piece. Press **R** off — left-hand exercise with the other staff's keys violet and unjudged. Turn both off: the strip states in one grey line that the hands are switched off, the drawn keyboard disappears, both staves fade together (there is nothing to play anywhere on the page), nothing is asked, nothing advances, and no request goes out (the good plan is kept — switching one letter back on resumes from it with zero requests). Two lit letters ARE "both hands"; there is no third button. What must change is what each step asks for, not necessarily how many steps there are — measured on the deployed service with Great Fairy Fountain: right keeps all 128 steps (that hand attacks at every position) but asks one key instead of two and shows the other key as violet context, while left drops to 88. The cursor must land on the notes of the chosen staff, an excluded key must not block and must not be judged, and lighting both letters again must restore the whole exercise. On a file whose notes sit on one staff only, that one letter is drawn and is a real switch — off pauses, with the line naming the staff; the absent letter is never drawn because that hand has no notes to offer. The staff dim follows the switch and nothing else: it regains color exactly when its own letter relights (resuming on one hand leaves the other hand's staff waiting grey), a one-staff file dims its single staff exactly when its only letter pauses, and leaving practice restores every staff — normal mode never shows a hand nobody switched off
